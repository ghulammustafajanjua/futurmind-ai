How to Make Money with AI YouTube Shorts in 2026

The real ways AI and faceless Shorts earn money, the numbers YouTube actually asks for, and the rule change coming in February 2027. Checked against YouTube's own help pages.

Published: October 30, 2026 · By Ghulam Mustafa Janjua

Many videos promise that AI Shorts will pay you every month after a few uploads. The truth is less exciting but more useful: Shorts can make money, but the views needed for ad income are very high, and most beginners earn their first money in other ways.

This guide is not about how to make the videos. If you need that, start with how to make faceless YouTube Shorts with AI for free. Here we only look at how the money works, using the rules on YouTube's official help pages as of October 2026.

Quick Answer: 6 Ways AI Shorts Can Earn Money

  1. Shorts ad revenue through the YouTube Partner Program (needs very high views).
  2. Fan funding such as Super Thanks and channel memberships (from 500 subscribers in some countries).
  3. YouTube Shopping affiliate commissions (only in selected countries).
  4. Brand deals and paid promotions.
  5. Selling Shorts-making services to businesses and other creators.
  6. Sending viewers to your own website or digital product.

Numbers 5 and 6 do not need any YouTube threshold, which is why they are often the fastest way for a beginner to earn the first dollar.

1. Shorts Ad Revenue (YouTube Partner Program)

Ad money from Shorts comes through the YouTube Partner Program (YPP). To join with Shorts, YouTube asks for:

Shorts route: 1,000 subscribers and 10 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days.

Long-video route: 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 qualified watch hours in the last 12 months.

Also needed: follow the monetization policies, live in a country where YPP is available (Pakistan and the UAE are both on YouTube's list), no active Community Guidelines strikes, 2-Step Verification turned on, advanced features access and one AdSense for YouTube account.

Watch hours from Shorts in the Shorts Feed do not count toward the 4,000 hours. Views from private, unlisted or deleted Shorts, and views from ad campaigns, do not count toward the 10 million.

How Shorts ad money is shared

Shorts ads play between videos in the Shorts Feed, so YouTube cannot link one ad to one Short. Instead it uses a shared pool:

  1. Each month, ad revenue from the Shorts Feed is added together.
  2. Part of it goes to a Creator Pool. Shorts that use music send part of their share to music licensing.
  3. Each monetizing creator gets a share of the pool based on their share of engaged Shorts views in each country.
  4. The creator keeps 45% of that allocated amount, whether or not music was used.

YouTube's own hypothetical example: in a country where Shorts ads earn $100,000 in a month and the Creator Pool is $90,000, a creator with 1% of the engaged views is allocated $900 and keeps 45%, which is $405. That example assumes 100 million engaged views in that country, of which the creator had 1 million. Real amounts change by country and month.

The big change from February 1, 2027

YouTube's Shorts monetization page says that starting February 1, 2027, a channel needs at least 10 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days to earn Shorts ad and YouTube Premium revenue for each month. Joining YPP once is no longer enough: Shorts earnings will depend on keeping that level of views. YPP members also need to accept the updated terms in YouTube Studio by January 31, 2027.

For a new channel this means Shorts ads should be treated as a long-term goal, not the first source of income.

2. Fan Funding from 500 Subscribers (Expanded YPP)

In many countries YouTube offers an earlier level of YPP. You can apply with:

  • 500 subscribers, 3 valid public uploads in the last 90 days and 3 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days, or
  • 500 subscribers, 3 valid public uploads in the last 90 days and 3,000 qualified watch hours in the last 12 months.

This level does not include ad revenue. It gives access to fan funding and Shopping features, such as channel memberships, Super Thanks, Super Chat and Super Stickers in live streams, and connecting your own merchandise store.

Country check: The United Arab Emirates, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Kuwait, Oman, Bahrain and India are on YouTube's list for the expanded program. Pakistan was not on the list when we checked in October 2026. Creators in Pakistan can still join the standard YPP at 1,000 subscribers.

3. YouTube Shopping Affiliate Program

With this program you tag products in your videos and earn a commission when viewers buy on the retailer's site. Commissions are paid through AdSense for YouTube within 60 to 120 days, and are reversed if the buyer returns the product.

To be invited, YouTube lists these minimum requirements:

  • Your channel is in YPP and meets the YPP subscriber threshold.
  • You are based in one of the supported countries.
  • Your channel is not a music channel and is not set as Made for Kids.

As of October 2026 the supported countries are Argentina, Brazil, Germany, India, Indonesia, Japan, Korea, Malaysia, Mexico, the Netherlands, the Philippines, Poland, Singapore, Taiwan, Thailand, the United Kingdom, the United States and Vietnam. The UAE and Pakistan are not included yet.

Important: YouTube does not allow you to share your YouTube Shopping affiliate links outside YouTube, for example on a website. Doing so can suspend your affiliate features.

4. Brand Deals and Paid Promotions

A Shorts channel with a clear niche and an engaged audience can be paid by brands to feature their product. This does not require YPP, but it does require trust: brands look at your niche, your audience and how real your engagement is.

If a video includes a sponsorship, endorsement or other branded content, you must follow YouTube's branded content policies and tell viewers about it.

5. Sell Shorts-Making Services

The skills you learn by making AI Shorts (scripts, AI visuals, voiceovers, editing and captions) are skills that businesses and busy creators pay for. You can offer them on freelance platforms such as Fiverr, or directly to local businesses.

Service ideas:

  • Turning a client's long video or podcast into Shorts
  • Writing Shorts scripts with strong hooks
  • Making faceless product or explainer Shorts for small businesses
  • Adding captions and simple edits to existing videos

Fiverr keeps 20% of every order, so price with that in mind. Use our Fiverr fee calculator to see what you actually keep, and read how to earn money on Fiverr with AI before you create your first gig.

6. Send Viewers to Your Own Website or Product

Shorts are good at reaching new people quickly. If you also have a blog, a free tool or a simple digital product, Shorts can introduce people to it. A website can earn from display ads and from affiliate programs that allow website links, and it keeps working even when a Short stops getting views.

Keep the content honest and useful. Each Short should still give value on its own instead of only advertising your site.

Rules That Decide Whether AI Shorts Can Earn

AI use does not automatically block monetization. What YouTube checks is whether the channel is original and authentic. These are the rules that matter most for AI and faceless channels:

Inauthentic content

On July 15, 2025, YouTube renamed its "repetitious content" policy to "inauthentic content" and clarified that it includes repetitive or mass-produced content. Content should be made for viewers' enjoyment or education, not only to get views. Uploading hundreds of near-identical AI Shorts from one template is exactly what this policy is about.

Reused content

If you borrow content from someone else, you need to change it significantly to make it your own. For Shorts revenue, YouTube does not count views of non-original Shorts, such as unedited clips from movies or TV shows, re-uploads of other creators' videos, or compilations with no original content added.

Fake views

Views from bots or other artificial methods are not counted for payments, and they put the whole channel at risk. Never buy views.

AI disclosure

You must disclose AI-generated or altered content that seems realistic, for example a realistic scene that never happened, or a real person appearing to say something they did not say. You do this with the AI use setting during upload. YouTube says disclosure alone does not limit a video's audience or its eligibility to earn money, but creators who keep failing to disclose can lose YPP access.

Music and longer Shorts

Shorts longer than one minute that contain claimed content, such as copyrighted music, are blocked and cannot be monetized. Use music you have the right to use.

A Realistic Plan for Beginners

  1. Pick one niche you can add real value to, such as AI tool tutorials, tech tips or facts you research properly.
  2. Make every Short different. Use AI to save time, but write or edit the script yourself and change the format between videos.
  3. Start earning outside YouTube's thresholds. Offer a simple Shorts service or link Shorts to your own site while the channel grows.
  4. Turn on "Get notified" in YouTube Studio under Earn, so YouTube emails you when you reach a threshold.
  5. Apply when eligible, and check the Earn section for the current rules in your country before applying.

Avoid any course or tool that promises guaranteed monthly income from automated Shorts. YouTube's rules are written specifically against mass-produced content, so those methods tend to fail at the review stage. Read how to spot online job scams for more warning signs.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you make money with AI YouTube Shorts?

Yes, but AI use alone is not what earns money. Your channel must follow YouTube's monetization policies, including the inauthentic content and reused content policies. Earnings can come from YPP, fan funding, affiliate programs where available, brand deals and selling Shorts services.

How many views do you need to monetize YouTube Shorts?

To join YPP with Shorts, you need 1,000 subscribers and 10 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days. From February 1, 2027, YouTube also requires at least 10 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days to earn Shorts ad and YouTube Premium revenue for each month.

How much of Shorts ad revenue do creators keep?

Monetizing creators keep 45% of the revenue allocated to them from the Shorts Creator Pool. The allocation is based on their share of engaged Shorts views in each country.

Does using music in Shorts reduce my earnings?

Music reduces the size of the overall Creator Pool because part of the revenue covers music licensing, but YouTube says using music does not change your own allocation from the pool or your 45% revenue share.

Do I have to disclose that my Shorts are made with AI?

You must disclose AI-generated or altered content that seems realistic, using the AI use setting in YouTube Studio. YouTube says disclosure alone does not limit a video's audience or its eligibility to earn money.

Final Thoughts

AI makes it faster to produce Shorts, but it does not change what YouTube pays for: original videos that real people want to watch. With the 10 million views rule arriving in February 2027, the smartest plan for a beginner is to treat Shorts as a way to build an audience and skills, and to earn the first money from services, your own website or fan funding while the channel grows.

YouTube can change these rules, so always confirm in YouTube Studio.
